## 1. What this system is for

It answers one question well: **what is in the store, and who took it.**

Every movement of stock is written down and can never be rubbed out. If
something is recorded wrongly, the correction is a new line that cancels the old
one — both stay visible. Nothing is ever quietly edited, and nothing is ever
deleted.

Stock levels are not typed in by anyone. They are added up from the movements
every time you look, so the number on the screen is always the sum of what
actually happened.

## 2. Signing in

Three taps, no typing.

1. **Which branch are you at?**
2. **Tap your own name** — your face on the list
3. **Enter your PIN** — 4 digits

Your PIN is yours. Everything you do has your name on it forever, so do not let
anyone else use your login and do not use theirs.

**Five wrong tries locks you out for fifteen minutes.** If that happens, or you
forget your PIN, the admin fixes it in seconds under **Logins**.

You stay signed in if you refresh or the tablet sleeps.

## 3. The one rule behind everything

> **The people who handle stock do not approve it.
> The people who approve it do not handle stock.**

That is why the screens look different depending on who you are. It is not the
system being awkward — it is the only thing that makes a check worth anything.
If the same person can do a thing and approve the thing, the approval means
nothing.

In practice:

| Who | What they do |
|---|---|
| **Kitchen / Cleaning** | Ask for stock. Say when it arrived. Hand back what is bad. Log what was wasted. Count their own shelf. |
| **Storekeeper** | Holds the store. Releases stock. Receives deliveries. Puts bad stock aside and sends it back. Asks for things to be bought. |
| **Management** | **Decides.** Approves returns, purchases, wastage. Verifies counts. Records credit notes. Reads the reports. |
| **Admin** | Hands out logins and sets up products, suppliers and branches. **Touches no stock at all.** |

## 4. If you work in the Kitchen

You have four screens: **Overview**, **Requests**, **What we have**, **Stock
counts** and **Wastage**.

### 4.1 Asking the store for stock

1. Open **Requests** → tap **Ask for stock**
2. **What do you need?** Start typing the product name — "chick" finds Chicken
   breast. The things your kitchen usually takes come up first.
3. **How much?** In the unit shown next to the box (g, ml, ea).
4. Add more items if you need them.
5. Say **when you need it** and add a note if it helps.
6. Tap **Send request**.

That is all you do. The storekeeper sees it and hands the stock over.

**If the store does not have enough**, you are told before you send, with the
figures. Send it anyway — the storekeeper decides what to do about the
shortfall. That is their job, not yours.

### 4.2 Saying it arrived

When the storekeeper releases your stock, the request appears under **Needs
me**.

1. Check the goods are physically in front of you
2. Tap **It came**

Only then is the request finished. **Do not confirm something you have not
actually received** — that is the whole point of the step.

### 4.3 Handing something back

If what arrived is bad — off fish, a split sack, the wrong grade — you hand it
back **from the request that delivered it**. There is no separate screen for
this and there should not be: the request is the thing you are complaining
about.

1. Open **Requests**, find that release
2. Tap **Return**
3. The drawer lists what was on that release, and says *at most N can go back*
4. Enter how much, choose **what is wrong with it**, add a note
5. Tap **Return it**

The stock leaves your section immediately. A manager approves it afterwards —
you do not wait for anybody. The store takes it from there.

**Three things to know:**

- You can only hand back **what was released to you on that request**, less
  anything you have already handed back, and never more than you still hold.
- **Returns close after seven days.** After a week nobody can honestly say the
  crate on your shelf is the one that came in on that release. After that, use
  **Wastage** (we spoiled it) instead.
- **Returning the whole of a release also confirms it arrived.** You cannot hand
  back something that never came, so the row is finished and leaves your queue.

### 4.4 Logging wastage

Something spoiled, burnt, dropped or broken.

1. Open **Wastage** → choose the product, the amount, and **why**
2. Add a photograph if you can — a photo taken at the time settles an argument
   later
3. Save

**The reason is not optional.** Wastage with a reason is honest waste and is
reported as waste. Stock that simply goes missing with no document is reported
as an **unexplained loss**, which is a very different conversation. Logging it
protects you.

### 4.5 Counting your own shelf

1. Open **Stock counts** → **Start a count** for your section
2. Count one product at a time, enter what you actually see

**The system never shows you what it expects.** That is deliberate — if it told
you, you would agree with it. Count the shelf, not the screen.

When you finish, the difference appears with a value. **A manager verifies it**;
you cannot verify your own count.

## 5. If you work in Cleaning

Exactly the same as the Kitchen, for the cleaning store:

- **Ask for stock** — soap, bleach, gloves
- **It came** when it arrives
- **Return** anything bad, against the release that brought it
- **Wastage** for a split drum or a broken mop
- **Stock counts** on your own shelf

You see the cleaning store and nothing else. The kitchen cannot reach into your
shelf and you cannot reach into theirs.

## 6. If you are the Storekeeper

You hold the store. Seven screens: **Requests**, **Receive delivery**,
**Deliveries**, **Stock on hand**, **Returns**, **Supplier returns**, **Purchase
orders** — plus counts and wastage.

### 6.1 Releasing stock to a section

1. **Requests** → the **Needs me** tab shows everything waiting
2. Tap **Release** on a request
3. You see what was asked for, and **what the store actually has** beside it
4. Change the amounts to what you are really handing over
5. Tap **Release these**

Stock moves off your shelf and onto theirs at that moment. The section then
confirms it arrived.

**If you cannot cover it in full:** release what you have. The system tells you
the shortfall and offers you a **purchase order** with those items already on
it. Only you can see whether the shelf is really empty, which is why only you
can raise one.

**You cannot release a request you raised yourself.** Somebody else has to.

### 6.2 Receiving a delivery

A lorry is at the door. **Receive delivery** asks four questions, one at a time.

| Step | What you do |
|---|---|
| **1. Which order?** | Pick the order this delivery is against and everything fills itself in. If nobody ordered it, tap **Nothing was ordered**. |
| **2. Who delivered it?** | The supplier, the invoice number and its date. Against an order, the supplier is fixed — a delivery from anybody else is its own delivery. |
| **3. What came?** | Each product, **in packs**, and the price per pack. |
| **4. Check and save** | Read it back, then **Save delivery**. |

**You never type grams.** You type "2 × 50 kg sack" and the line tells you
*"That is 100 kg onto the shelf"* underneath. If that sentence looks wrong, the
pack is wrong.

**The price is checked while you type it.** If it has moved more than 10% since
last time, an amber note says so, with the old price: *"That is up 32% on last
time. Last from this supplier: LKR 45,880.00 a pack."* It **does not stop you**
— the lorry has gone, and refusing the delivery would only make the stock figure
wrong as well. Record it. It appears in **Reports → Price movement** for
management.

Orders management has not approved yet are shown greyed out. You cannot receive
against them until management says yes.

**After you press Save** — see [10.1](#101-what-happens-when-you-save-a-delivery).

### 6.2a Looking a delivery up afterwards

**Deliveries** is the record of everything that has arrived at this branch,
newest first. Search by supplier name or invoice number.

Open one and you see exactly what was booked in: every line, the packs, what
each pack cost, what it put on the shelf, and the total. If any of it has since
gone back to the supplier, the line says so — so *"did we get a credit for the
bad half of that delivery?"* is answered on the delivery itself.

It is **read-only on purpose**. A delivery on the record is corrected with a
reversal — a second entry that cancels it and stays beside it — never by editing
it. Ask management.

Management sees this screen too. It is where *"what did we pay for that last
time?"* gets answered, which is the question the price report raises.

### 6.3 When a section hands something back

Your **Returns** screen has three parts:

| Part | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Waiting for management** | Hand-backs nobody has approved yet. Nothing is blocked by this — the goods are already with you. |
| **Waiting to go back** | What is sitting in **quarantine** right now, and what it is worth. Anything already on an ask is marked *already asked about*, so you are never invited to ask about the same crate twice |
| **Came back off the floor** | The full record of what sections have handed back |

**Quarantine** is a separate shelf for bad stock. Nothing is ever issued out of
it. Stock leaves it exactly once — when it goes back to the supplier, or is
written off. That is the point: bad stock sitting in your main balance is bad
stock the kitchen can ask for again tomorrow.

**Bad stock on your own shelf** — a sack that was wet when it arrived, never
went to any section — uses the button at the top: **Something on our own shelf
is bad**. That is the only case that has no release behind it, because it came
in on a *delivery*.

### 6.4 Sending it back to the supplier

From **Returns**, tap **Send it back to [supplier]**. The Supplier returns
screen opens **already filled in** — the delivery, the reason, the packs, the
credit. You do not retype anything.

1. Check the delivery is the right one. *It is a best guess* — nothing ties a
   crate to an invoice, so the system suggests the most recent delivery of that
   product that still has packs left to return. Change it if you know better.
2. Check the quantities. **They are counted in packs**, because that is what a
   credit note is written in — so a part pack is normal. If the cleaning store
   handed back 1 litre of bleach and bleach comes in a 5 L can, the box reads
   **0.2**, and the line underneath it confirms **= 1 L**. You can only send
   back **what quarantine actually holds**.

   **Only what is in quarantine is listed** — usually one or two lines, not the
   whole invoice. You could not claim for the rest anyway. If more of the same
   delivery turns out to be bad, hand it back to the store and it appears here.
3. Tap **Raise the return**.

Then you **wait for management to approve it** — it is money, so it is their
decision. Nothing has moved yet: the crate is still in your building and the
stock figure still says so.

When they approve and the lorry actually takes it away:

4. Tap **It has gone**

**That is the moment the stock leaves quarantine**, and it is the only step in
the whole return process where a supplier return touches your stock figures.

Management records the credit note later.

### 6.5 Asking for something to be bought

**Purchase orders** → **Raise a purchase**. Three questions:

| Step | What you do |
|---|---|
| **1. What** | Open *What the shelf says* — everything already below its reorder point — and add what you need. Search for anything else by name. |
| **2. How much** | In packs, with a rough price per pack. A guess is fine. |
| **3. Why** | **Management decides on this sentence, so write it.** "Down to two sacks, Saturday is busy" beats "need rice". |

Then it goes to management. You cannot approve your own purchase — you ask for
the money, they spend it.

When the goods arrive, receive them **against that order** (step 1 of Receive
delivery). A short delivery leaves the order open for the rest.

## 7. If you are Management

You see everything and you **decide**. You do not hand stock over, put stock
aside, or load a lorry — those are the store's job, and if you did them too your
approval would be approving your own work.

### 7.1 What is waiting for you

| Screen | What you decide |
|---|---|
| **Returns** | Approve hand-backs from sections |
| **Supplier returns** | Approve or reject goods going back; record the credit note afterwards |
| **Purchase orders** | Approve or reject spending |
| **Wastage** | Approve what was written off |
| **Stock counts** | Verify counts other people performed |
| **Reports** | The five reports — nobody else sees these |

### 7.2 What the Returns screen is for you

A section handed something back. **The stock already moved** when they did it —
they did not wait for you, and they should not have to.

There is nothing to approve here. Open **Returns** to see **what is in
quarantine and what it is worth** — money the restaurant has paid for and
cannot use, which is worth watching — and the record of what has come back. The
decision comes to you on the next screen, when the storekeeper asks for it.

### 7.3 Answering the store's question

Open **Supplier returns**. The top of the screen shows the four stages and who
does each:

```
ASKED           DECIDED         GONE OR BINNED   SETTLED
Storekeeper     Management      Storekeeper      Management
what is in      each line:      whichever you    credit note
quarantine      back or bin     said             ↑ no stock move
                                ↑ stock moves here
```

**Waiting for your decision** lists the asks, each with its lines and what each
line is worth. For every line, tap one of two buttons:

| | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Send it back** | We claim a credit from the supplier for it |
| **Bin it** | We throw it away and write the value off |

Then **Save the decision**. You cannot save a half-answered ask — every line has
to go somewhere, because a line nobody answered is stock that sits in quarantine
for a month with nobody claiming it and nobody refusing it.

Both buttons are money. One asks a supplier for a credit; the other writes the
value off. Neither is preselected, and neither is styled as the safe option.

You will not see an *It has gone* or *Bin them* button. Those are the
storekeeper doing what you decided — putting goods on a lorry, or in a bin — and
they are the one standing next to them. Decided rows read *"Waiting for the
store to send it"* or *"to bin it"* until they do.

### 7.4 Recording what the supplier did

Weeks later the supplier responds. On the sent return, choose one:

| Button | Use it when | It asks for |
|---|---|---|
| **Credit note received** | They allowed money | The note number **and** the amount |
| **Replaced** | Fresh goods arrived | Nothing — the replacement comes in as a normal delivery |
| **Nothing back** | Written off | Nothing |

This is only for lines you sent **back to the supplier**. Anything you marked
**Bin it** needs no settlement — it was written off the moment you decided, and
the store binning it is the end of it.

A credit without its note number is refused. The reason to record a credit is to
check it against the statement, and a credit you cannot find on the statement is
just a note in a diary.

If they allowed **less** than was asked for, the screen shows the shortfall
beside it. That gap is the number worth arguing about.

### 7.5 Approving a purchase

**Purchase orders** shows what the storekeeper has asked for, with the reason
they wrote and the estimated cost. **Approve** or **Reject** — rejecting asks
for a reason.

Only you can do this. Every purchase comes to you, whatever it costs.

### 7.6 The five reports

| Report | The question it answers |
|---|---|
| **Usage variance** | Are we using more of something than the recipes say we should? |
| **Shrinkage** | What has gone missing with no document behind it? |
| **Price movement** | Which supplier prices have jumped? |
| **Wastage by reason** | What are we throwing away, and why? |
| **Stock-outs** | What ran out, and when? |

**Shrinkage and Wastage are deliberately different reports.** Waste that
somebody logged honestly, with a reason, appears under Wastage and **never**
under Shrinkage. If documented spoilage ever shows up as unexplained loss, the
report is wrong — tell us. A report that accuses people over a crate of lettuce
is one nobody opens by week three.

### 7.7 What you can see but not do

You can open **Requests** and read every request and release in the branch. You
will not find a **Release** button, and there is no *Needs me* tab for you —
nothing on that screen is ever waiting for you. It is a record, not a queue.

Same on **Returns**: no shelf drawer, no *Send it back*.

If the storekeeper is away, **the answer is a second storekeeper login**, not a
manager reaching past the control. An admin creates one in under a minute.

## 8. Returns, explained properly

This is the part people find hardest, so here it is twice.

### 8.1 As a story

> Tuesday. A box of fish comes in from Negombo Fish Supply. Sunil books the
> delivery in and it sits in the store.
>
> Wednesday, Chaminda asks for 5 kg of it. Sunil releases it. Chaminda taps
> *It came*.
>
> Chaminda opens the box. It smells wrong. He opens **Requests**, finds the
> release from Wednesday, taps **Return**, enters 5 kg, picks *Below the quality
> agreed*, and writes "off smell on opening". **The fish leaves the kitchen
> straight away** and goes onto the quarantine shelf. He does not wait for
> anybody — the fish is bad now.
>
> Sunil opens **Returns**, sees the fish waiting to go back, and taps **Ask
> management what to do**. The next screen is already filled in — the delivery
> it came in on, the reason, the packs, and what they owe. He taps **Raise the
> return**. **The fish has still not moved.** It is in quarantine, it is still
> ours, and the stock figure says so.
>
> Nuwan opens **Supplier returns** and sees one question: *the fish — send it
> back, or bin it?* He taps **Send it back** and saves. **Still nothing moves.**
>
> Friday the supplier's van comes. Sunil hands over the box and taps **It has
> gone**. **Now** the fish leaves quarantine. It is out of the building and out
> of our stock.
>
> Three weeks later a credit note arrives for LKR 4,200. Nuwan opens the return,
> taps **Credit note received**, and types the number and the amount. Finished.

### 8.2 As a table

| # | Step | Who | Screen | Does stock move? |
|---|---|---|---|:--:|
| 1 | Hand it back | Kitchen / Cleaning | Requests → **Return** | **YES** — leaves the section, enters quarantine |
| 2 | Ask: claim it, or bin it? | Storekeeper | Returns → *Ask management what to do* | No |
| 3 | **Answer it, line by line** | **Management** | Supplier returns → *Send it back* / *Bin it* | No |
| 4 | Carry out the answer | Storekeeper | Supplier returns → *It has gone* **or** *Bin them* | **YES** — leaves quarantine |
| 5 | Record the credit note | **Management** | Supplier returns → *Credit note received* | No — only for what went back |

**Only steps 1 and 4 move stock.** Everything else is paperwork. If you remember
nothing else about returns, remember that — it is what stops people thinking the
goods have gone when they are still sitting on the quarantine shelf.

> **Where does the stock actually end up?** There are four possible endings —
> credit, replacement, nothing back, or written off after a rejection — and
> [What actually happens to stock the kitchen sends back](what-happens-to-returned-stock.md)
> follows one crate through all of them, hour by hour.

### 8.3 Storekeeper vs Management, side by side

**On Returns:**

| | Storekeeper | Management |
|---|---|---|
| See what is in quarantine, and its value | ✅ | ✅ (information only) |
| Put the store's own bad stock into quarantine | ✅ | ❌ |
| Ask management what to do with it | ✅ | ❌ |

**On Supplier returns:**

| | Storekeeper | Management |
|---|---|---|
| See every ask and where it has got to | ✅ | ✅ |
| Pick the delivery and write the ask up | ✅ | ❌ |
| **Answer it — send back, or bin, line by line** | ❌ | ✅ |
| Mark it gone when the lorry takes it | ✅ | ❌ |
| Bin the lines management said to bin | ✅ | ❌ |
| **Record the credit note** | ❌ | ✅ |

Read down the two columns: **they never overlap on the same row.** One person
handles the goods, the other decides about the money. That is the whole design.

### 8.4 Two different things people mix up

| | **Return** | **Wastage** |
|---|---|---|
| What happened | The supplier gave us something bad | We spoiled, burnt, dropped or broke it |
| Whose fault | Theirs | Ours |
| Where it goes | Quarantine, then back to them | Thrown away |
| Money | We want a credit | The money is gone |
| Screen | Requests → Return | Wastage |

If you are not sure: **did it arrive bad, or did we ruin it?** That is the whole
question.

## 9. Things the system will not let you do

If any of these ever work, tell us — something is wrong.

| You cannot | Because |
|---|---|
| Edit or delete anything on the stock record | It is the record. A correction is a reversal that sits beside the original |
| Release a request you raised yourself | Two people, or it is not a check |
| Confirm a delivery you released yourself | Same reason |
| Verify a count you performed yourself | Otherwise the count checks nothing |
| Approve your own return or purchase | Same reason |
| Take stock negative | The shelf cannot hold less than nothing |
| Return more than was released to you | A return is measured against the release that delivered it |
| Return something released over a week ago | Nobody can honestly say it is the same stock. Use Wastage |
| Send goods back before management approves | It is money |
| See another branch's stock | You work at one branch |

## 10. When something goes wrong

### 10.1 What happens when you save a delivery

**If it works:** the screen stays where it is and shows **Delivery recorded** —
the supplier, the delivery number, the total, and for each line *what went in*
and *what is on the shelf now*. That last figure is read straight off the stock
record, so it is proof, not a promise.

From there: **Receive another delivery**, or **Stock on hand** to see the whole
shelf.

**If it fails:** a red panel says **That did not save**, with the reason, and:

> **Nothing was recorded.** The delivery is exactly as you left it — fix what
> the message says and press Save again.

That is literally true. The delivery and the stock movements are written
together or not at all — there is no halfway. Your typing is untouched.

**If the tablet loses connection**, press Save again when it comes back. The
system recognises it as the same delivery and will not record it twice. This is
also why pressing Save twice quickly is safe.

### 10.2 Common messages

| Message | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| *"Session expired, sign in again"* | You were idle a long time | Sign in again. Nothing is lost |
| *"You do not have access to that location"* | Wrong branch | Switch branch at the top right |
| *"This action needs one of: …"* | That job is not yours | The message names whose it is |
| *"Management has not approved this return yet"* | You tried to send too early | Wait for the decision |
| *"That section is not one of yours"* | You tried to work in another room | Use your own section |
| *"…has been switched off"* | That section or product is retired | Ask an admin |
| *"Too old to return"* | Over seven days since the release | Use Wastage instead |

### 10.3 Who to ask

| Problem | Ask |
|---|---|
| Forgot your PIN, or locked out | **Admin** — fixed in seconds under Logins |
| A product, supplier or pack size is missing or wrong | **Admin** |
| Something was recorded wrongly and needs reversing | **Management** |
| A number looks wrong on a report | **Management**, then TriniphiX |
| The screen will not do something you think it should | Check [section 9](#9-things-the-system-will-not-let-you-do) first — it may be deliberate |
